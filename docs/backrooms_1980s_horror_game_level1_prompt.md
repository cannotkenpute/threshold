# GAME DESIGN PROMPT — BACKROOMS 1980s FIRST-PERSON HORROR

## Project Overview

Design a **web-based 3D first-person horror game** with a **pixelized retro visual style**, inspired by late-1980s computer graphics, VHS footage, early 3D rendering, and analog research equipment.

The game takes place in the **1980s**.

The player is a **research scientist** working for a secretive government-backed research program studying a newly discovered extradimensional space informally called **The Backrooms**.

A first research team was sent inside the dimension.

They never returned.

The player was originally assigned as the **backup scientist** and was never expected to enter the dimension. After communication with the original team is lost, the research program orders the player to enter the Backrooms, locate the missing researchers, recover their research, and determine what happened.

The mission begins as a controlled tethered expedition.

It does not stay controlled for long.

---

# Core Game Identity

## Genre

- First-person psychological horror
- Exploration
- Survival
- Environmental storytelling
- Light inventory management
- Mystery
- Analog / retro science fiction

## Platform

The game should be playable directly in a modern web browser.

The visual presentation should intentionally resemble an experimental late-1980s / early-1990s 3D game rather than modern photorealism.

## Visual Style

Use:

- Low-resolution pixelized textures
- Low-poly 3D geometry
- Dithered lighting
- Vertex-style lighting
- Limited color palettes
- Slight texture warping
- Film grain
- VHS noise
- CRT distortion on terminals and cameras
- Occasional screen tearing or analog interference
- Chunky first-person item models
- Retro UI elements
- Minimal anti-aliasing
- Fog and darkness to hide distant geometry

The Backrooms should feel simultaneously artificial, familiar, and physically impossible.

---

# Setting

## The Research Program

A classified experimental physics program has recently discovered a stable dimensional anomaly.

Researchers do not fully understand what the dimension is.

Internal documents refer to it using technical names such as:

**Dimensional Test Environment 04**

or

**Nonlocal Interior Space**

But members of the research team have started informally calling it:

**The Backrooms**

The term originated because the dimension appears to contain endless rooms, maintenance corridors, empty offices, storage areas, utility spaces, and other environments that resemble forgotten areas behind normal buildings.

The year should feel unmistakably 1980s.

The laboratory contains:

- CRT monitors
- Reel-to-reel recording equipment
- Dot-matrix printers
- Cassette recorders
- Analog clocks
- Oscilloscopes
- Paper research folders
- Rotary phones
- Fluorescent lighting
- Large industrial computers
- Wired intercoms
- Mechanical switches
- Magnetic tape storage

There are no smartphones, GPS devices, digital maps, or modern wireless communication.

---

# Player Character

The player is a research scientist assigned as the backup member of the Backrooms exploration team.

The player should not be a soldier.

They have scientific training but very limited combat experience.

The player enters the Backrooms carrying basic expedition equipment.

Suggested starting equipment:

- Flashlight
- Spare batteries
- Cassette recorder
- Notebook
- Mechanical compass
- Emergency medical kit
- Small tool kit
- Research camera
- Rope tether
- Identification badge

The player has been instructed to prioritize:

1. Locating the missing research team.
2. Recovering their research notes.
3. Recording environmental information.
4. Returning through the original entrance.

---

# Story Opener

## Opening Sequence — Research Facility

The game begins inside the research facility shortly before the player enters the Backrooms.

The player walks through a dim underground laboratory toward the anomaly chamber.

The facility should feel active but tense.

Researchers avoid eye contact.

Several workstations are abandoned.

A wall-mounted clock ticks loudly.

A CRT monitor displays a frozen video frame from the original expedition.

The player hears fragments of conversation from nearby scientists.

Example ambient dialogue:

> "We lost the signal six hours ago."

> "The tether should have pulled them back."

> "It didn't."

> "There shouldn't be that much space inside."

The player enters a preparation room.

A supervisor gives the mission briefing through an intercom or behind reinforced glass.

The player is told that the original four-person research team entered the anomaly approximately twelve hours earlier.

Contact was lost after forty-seven minutes.

The final transmission contained distorted audio and a repeated message:

> "The rooms changed."

The supervisor insists that the mission is still classified as a recovery operation rather than a rescue mission.

The player is equipped with a safety harness connected to a long industrial rope.

The rope is physically anchored inside the research facility.

The plan is simple:

Enter.

Follow the tether.

Locate the team.

Return.

---

# Entering the Backrooms

The dimensional entrance should not initially look supernatural.

It may resemble a distorted doorway, an unfinished maintenance opening, or a rectangular section of space where perspective behaves incorrectly.

When the player crosses the threshold:

- Laboratory sounds immediately disappear.
- The rope continues behind the player.
- Fluorescent humming becomes the dominant sound.
- The player's compass begins behaving inconsistently.
- Radio communication becomes heavily distorted.

The player enters a large empty yellow room.

The environment contains:

- Yellow wallpaper
- Damp carpet
- Fluorescent ceiling lights
- Repeating support columns
- Empty doorways
- Unnatural architectural angles

The player can still see the anomaly entrance behind them.

For approximately thirty seconds, the mission appears to be functioning normally.

Then the player hears a distant metallic noise.

The lights flicker.

The rope suddenly becomes slack.

The player pulls the rope toward themselves.

The severed end slides across the carpet.

The rope has not been cut cleanly.

It looks as if several meters of it simply disappeared.

The player turns around.

The entrance is gone.

There is only another yellow wall.

The first objective appears:

## OBJECTIVE: FIND A WAY BACK

Then it changes after several seconds:

## OBJECTIVE: LOCATE THE RESEARCH TEAM

---

# LEVEL 1 — THE YELLOW SECTOR

## Level Purpose

The first level introduces:

- Exploration
- Environmental storytelling
- Item collection
- Battery management
- Diary entries
- Basic survival mechanics
- Spatial uncertainty
- The first signs that something else is inside the Backrooms

The level should initially feel empty rather than immediately dangerous.

Fear should come from uncertainty.

The player should repeatedly question whether they saw or heard something.

---

# Level Layout

The Yellow Sector is a large semi-open maze composed of interconnected rooms and corridors.

The environment should not be procedurally random during the first level.

Instead, it should be deliberately designed so that the player can slowly learn landmarks while still feeling lost.

Suggested areas include:

## 1. Entry Room

The initial yellow room.

Contains:

- Broken rope
- One dropped flashlight battery
- Scratches on the wall
- Small pieces of scientific equipment

The player discovers that one of the missing researchers may have passed through this exact location.

---

## 2. Fluorescent Corridor

A long corridor where every fluorescent light creates a slightly different buzzing frequency.

One light repeatedly turns off as the player approaches it.

When the player looks backward, the corridor appears longer than before.

This should be the first obvious indication that geometry can change.

---

## 3. Abandoned Equipment Station

A temporary research checkpoint constructed by the missing team.

Contains:

- Folding table
- Portable scientific instruments
- Empty food wrappers
- Batteries
- Hand-drawn map
- Cassette recorder
- First diary entry

The equipment suggests that the missing team stayed here much longer than expected.

---

## 4. Carpet Flood Area

Several interconnected rooms where the carpet is soaked with shallow water.

The player hears footsteps whenever they move through the water.

Occasionally another splash can be heard several seconds after the player stops walking.

No creature should be clearly visible yet.

---

## 5. Dark Maintenance Hall

A narrow service corridor where many fluorescent lights have failed.

This area requires flashlight use.

The player can find useful supplies but must spend battery power to explore it.

---

## 6. Observation Room

A room with unusual marks and research equipment left by the original team.

One wall contains dozens of arrows drawn in marker.

Most point in different directions.

Written underneath:

> DO NOT TRUST THE HALLWAYS.

---

## 7. Final Corridor

A long corridor leading toward what appears to be another research camp.

This section contains the first undeniable encounter with something alive.

---

# Exploration Mechanics

The first level should reward curiosity.

Players can discover:

- Research notes
- Cassette recordings
- Polaroid photographs
- Scientific measurements
- Personal diary entries
- Hand-drawn maps
- Symbols
- Equipment
- Survival supplies

Important clues should remain optional whenever possible.

Players who explore more thoroughly should understand the story earlier.

---

# Diary Entry System

Diary entries are one of the primary storytelling mechanics.

The missing researchers gradually document their experience.

The entries should begin professional and scientific.

Over time they become increasingly personal, confused, and frightened.

---

# Missing Research Team

Create four missing researchers.

Suggested roles:

## Dr. Evelyn Mercer

Expedition leader.

Calm, methodical, and highly respected.

Initially refuses to describe the Backrooms using supernatural language.

---

## Dr. Samuel Reed

Spatial physicist.

Obsessed with measuring the geometry of the Backrooms.

First person to realize that distance and direction are unreliable.

---

## Dr. Helen Park

Biologist and medical specialist.

Begins documenting unusual organic material discovered inside the dimension.

---

## Daniel Cole

Technical engineer responsible for communication equipment and the tether system.

Becomes increasingly convinced that something is following the group.

---

# Diary Entry 01

Found at the abandoned equipment station.

**Dr. Mercer — Expedition Log**

> Entry time: 09:42.
>
> The environment remains stable.
>
> Temperature is approximately twenty-one degrees Celsius.
>
> Humidity is unusually high.
>
> We have traveled approximately four hundred meters from the entrance.
>
> Visual landmarks remain repetitive, but the tether continues to provide a reliable path back.
>
> Reed believes the internal dimensions of the structure exceed the physical volume recorded by the external sensors.
>
> We will continue another two hundred meters before returning.

This entry should make the expedition sound completely controlled.

---

# Diary Entry 02

Found farther inside the level.

**Samuel Reed — Personal Notes**

> Something is wrong with the measurements.
>
> I marked the eastern wall twenty minutes ago.
>
> We walked north.
>
> We turned left three times.
>
> We found the same mark.
>
> That should be impossible.
>
> Mercer says I made two marks by accident.
>
> I didn't.

---

# Diary Entry 03

Optional.

Found beside damaged equipment.

**Daniel Cole — Tape Recording**

The recording begins with heavy static.

Cole whispers:

> There's someone walking behind us.
>
> Mercer says it's the echo.
>
> Carpet doesn't echo.
>
> I stopped walking.
>
> It kept going.

A loud sound occurs somewhere in the recording.

The tape ends.

---

# Survival Items

Level 1 should introduce basic survival resources.

Possible items:

## Flashlight Batteries

Used to power the flashlight.

Should be limited enough to encourage careful exploration.

---

## Medical Supplies

Used to recover from injuries later in the game.

Level 1 should contain at least one medical kit even if combat has not fully begun.

---

## Almond Water

A strange bottle discovered at one of the missing team's camps.

The research team originally brought ordinary water.

This bottle is unfamiliar.

Its label appears homemade.

The player does not yet know its significance.

It can restore a small amount of health or reduce a hidden stress / fear mechanic.

---

## Energy Bar

Simple food item.

May restore stamina.

---

## Spare Cassette

Allows the player to save recordings or recover additional audio logs depending on the final game mechanics.

---

## Flares

Rare light source.

Useful for illuminating large rooms or marking routes.

---

# Inventory Design

The player should have limited inventory capacity.

Suggested system:

- 6 primary inventory slots
- 2 equipment slots
- Notes do not consume inventory space
- Small identical items can stack

The player should regularly choose between survival supplies and exploratory equipment.

---

# Psychological Horror Mechanics

Avoid excessive jump scares.

Fear should primarily come from environmental behavior.

Examples:

- A doorway disappears after the player walks through it.
- A hallway becomes longer.
- A previously empty room contains a chair when revisited.
- Fluorescent lights switch off one at a time behind the player.
- Footsteps occur when the player stops moving.
- A distant human silhouette appears briefly and vanishes.
- A wall contains writing that was not there earlier.
- The player's compass points toward the nearest light instead of north.
- The sound of the research facility radio occasionally appears even though communication has been lost.

---

# First Entity Introduction

Do not reveal the main creature immediately.

During most of Level 1, the entity should only be suggested.

Possible signs:

- Footsteps
- Shadows
- Movement beyond doorways
- Scratching sounds
- Objects displaced between visits
- Breathing heard through ventilation
- Distant silhouette
- Broken research equipment

Near the end of the level, the player finally sees a humanoid figure standing far down a corridor.

The figure resembles one of the missing researchers.

It does not move.

The player may initially believe they found a survivor.

When the player approaches, the fluorescent lights begin shutting off from the far end of the hallway toward the player.

The silhouette disappears.

Seconds later, rapid footsteps begin moving toward the player from the darkness.

The player must run.

---

# First Chase Sequence

The first chase should be short but intense.

The player runs through several previously explored rooms.

However, the layout has changed.

Doorways appear where walls previously existed.

Some familiar passages are missing.

The player must follow environmental clues such as:

- Emergency flares
- Arrows left by the research team
- Flickering lights
- Open doors

The entity should remain mostly obscured.

Only partial glimpses should be visible.

Examples:

- Long arms crossing a doorway
- A silhouette turning a corner
- A distorted human face visible for a single frame
- Hands reaching from darkness

The player eventually reaches a heavy maintenance door and slams it shut.

The footsteps stop.

Silence.

---

# Level 1 Final Discovery

Behind the maintenance door is another temporary research camp.

Unlike the first camp, this one has been destroyed.

Equipment is scattered across the floor.

Blood is present, but no bodies are visible.

The player finds a final cassette recording.

---

# Final Cassette Recording

**Dr. Evelyn Mercer**

The recording begins calmly but quickly deteriorates.

> We lost the entrance.
>
> Cole checked the tether.
>
> It wasn't broken.
>
> It simply ended.
>
> Reed says the rooms are reorganizing around us.
>
> Helen thinks something is reacting to our movement.
>
> We have decided to establish another camp and wait for rescue.
>
> If another team finds this recording...
>
> do not follow our markers.
>
> Something else has started using them.

A distant voice can be heard in the recording.

It sounds exactly like Mercer.

It says:

> Evelyn.

Mercer stops speaking.

The recording continues for several seconds.

Then:

> That isn't me.

The tape ends.

---

# Level 1 Ending

After listening to the recording, the player notices a doorway behind the research camp.

The doorway was not there earlier.

Beyond it is a completely different environment.

The yellow wallpaper ends.

The next area appears darker and more industrial.

A sign written by one of the researchers is attached beside the doorway:

> LEVEL 2?

Underneath, someone has written:

> DON'T GO BACK.

The fluorescent lights behind the player begin turning off.

One by one.

The player enters the doorway.

The door closes.

Cut to black.

Display:

# LEVEL 1 COMPLETE

Then:

## THE BACKROOMS ARE NOT EMPTY.

---

# Core Questions Introduced by Level 1

The first level should leave the player wondering:

- What exactly are the Backrooms?
- Why does the geometry change?
- What happened to the original research team?
- Why did the tether disappear?
- Is something intentionally trapping explorers?
- Is the creature human?
- Can the Backrooms imitate voices or people?
- How long has this dimension existed?
- Did the research program actually know more than it told the player?

Do not answer these questions during Level 1.

---

# Tone

The tone should combine:

- 1980s classified science
- Analog horror
- Liminal spaces
- Psychological horror
- Scientific mystery
- Isolation
- Survival
- Cosmic horror

The player should constantly feel that the Backrooms operate according to rules.

The terrifying part is that the player does not know those rules yet.

---

# Design Rule

Do not rely on constant monsters or jump scares.

The primary source of fear should be the environment itself.

The Backrooms should behave like a place that notices the player.

The longer the player explores, the more obvious it becomes that the dimension may not simply be a location.

It may be observing them.
